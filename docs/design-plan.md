# Red October: A Design Plan

A fork of *Social Democracy: An Alternate History* set in the Russian Revolution, 1917–1921.

Oct 6, 2026 · Eva

## Concept

The player chooses one of Russia's three non-Bolshevik socialist parties (the Mensheviks, the SRs or the Left SRs) and leads it from the February Revolution (March 1917) to December 1921, trying to build a democratic socialist Russia without a White military dictatorship or Bolshevik one-party rule.

**Why the Mensheviks come first.** They were Russia's social democrats, in the same International as the SPD, so the original game's systems carry over almost one to one: factions, advisors, elections, coalition partners. They also had the steepest fall of any party. In June 1917 they held 248 delegates at the First Congress of Soviets and the largest bloc on its executive (123 of 320). By November they won 3% in the Constituent Assembly election, nearly half of it from Georgia. By January 1922 their leaders were in prison or exile. That gap is the game.

**The core tension.** In 1917 the party shares power without controlling it: its ministers own the war, the bread crisis and the land delay. After October it must oppose the Bolsheviks without helping the Whites. Every choice trades worker support against legality, and party unity against both.

**Shape of a run.**

- Two-week turns from March 1917 until the calendar reform of February 1918, then monthly turns to December 1921: 69 turns, close to the original's span (see Turns and time).
- Turns follow the Gregorian (New Style) calendar. Event text gives both dates for 1917, so the October Revolution (25 Oct Old Style) lands in the first turn of November 1917.
- One playthrough should still take an hour or less.

**Win and lose.** The run ends early on a White victory (Kornilov in September 1917, or the Whites reaching Moscow in 1919) or when the party is destroyed. Otherwise it ends in December 1921 and the ending depends on the state of the soviets, the party's legality and the economy (see Endings).

The SRs and Left SRs are full campaigns with their own factions, advisors and goals, and the three parties can ally with each other (see Playable parties and alliances).

## Playable parties and alliances

The start menu offers three campaigns on one shared timeline; each party has its own goal, factions, advisors and signature mechanic, and the other two are possible allies. Most events are shared, with options that change by party; each campaign adds about ten events of its own.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |

### SRs: factions and advisors

Factions: **Right SRs** (Avksentiev, Breshko-Breshkovskaya; Kerensky close to them), **Centre** (Chernov), **Right Centre** (Gots, Zenzinov), and the **Left SRs** (Spiridonova, Kamkov, Natanson), who leave in November 1917 unless the player keeps them. In 1919 an event can create the **Ufa delegation** (Volsky), which rejects armed struggle against the Bolsheviks and historically formed the Minority SR Party in October 1919.

### Left SRs: factions and advisors

Until the split the player controls a faction, not a party: Left SR cards raise its strength inside the SR party and the split can come early (the First Congress of Soviets), on schedule (November 1917) or never. After the founding congress (early December 1917, 116 delegates from 99 organisations), the party has its own currents: the **Kamkov–Spiridonova leadership**, **pro-coalition** Left SRs (Kolegaev, Natanson, later the Revolutionary Communists) and **pro-Bolshevik** Left SRs (later the Narodnik Communists, who joined the Bolsheviks in November 1918).

### Alliances

Relations with the other two playable parties, and with the Bolsheviks, run from 0 to 100 and unlock three levels.

| Level | Needs | Unlocks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cooperation | Relation 40 | Coordinated votes in one arena, joint statements |
| Bloc | Relation 60 and compatible lines on the war and the soviets | Joint election lists (votes pooled, seats split by strength), a shared soviet presidium, the ally's event choices lean your way |
| Coalition | Relation 75 and a shared government or soviet majority | Shared ministries; one of the ally's advisors joins your advisor deck; the ally's dissent feeds your own |

Starting relations follow history: the Mensheviks and SRs begin as a bloc (the 1917 Soviet majority); the Left SRs begin inside the SR party; the Left SRs and Bolsheviks can form the December 1917 coalition. New blocs are possible: an anti-Brest front of Mensheviks and Left SRs (Martov and Kamkov both spoke against ratification in March 1918), or an all-socialist government of all three with the Bolsheviks (the Vikzhel talks).

Alliances break at the historical fault lines unless relations are high: Brest-Litovsk (Left SRs), armed struggle and Komuch (the SRs against the Mensheviks' August 1918 ban), the Left SR uprising of July 1918, and Kolchak's coup. A party the player does not lead follows its historical path, but a strong bloc can turn it: a Menshevik ally can talk the SRs out of joining Komuch, and a Left SR in coalition can stop the July 1918 assassination of the German ambassador.

### What changes in the engine

- A `Q.player_party` variable set on the party-select screen, and `view-if` on every party-only card, advisor and event.
- The original hard-codes the SPD (`workers_spd`, `spd_relation`). The player's party gets a fixed slot, `you`, labelled with the chosen party's name in charts; the other two playable parties keep their own slots in `Q.parties`.
- Shared events use `choose-if` on options that only some parties can take.
- `Q.factions` is set per party at the start, so the faction count can differ (the engine already loops over the list).

## Turns and time

Turns last two weeks until the New Calendar event in February 1918, then one month to the end: 22 two-week turns (March 1917 to January 1918) and 47 monthly turns (February 1918 to December 1921), 69 in all.

**Why the calendar reform.** Soviet Russia adopted the Gregorian calendar in February 1918: the day after 31 January was 14 February. The event fires in the first February 1918 turn in every line of play, just after the Constituent Assembly crisis, so the 1917 revolution plays in fine grain and the Civil War in broad strokes. Its text adapts to whoever governs: in a line where the Assembly survives, the Assembly's government issues the decree instead.

**What changes in the engine.**

- A turn-length variable (half a month, then one month) and a half-month marker. `Q.time`, `Q.month` and `Q.year` advance only when a month completes.
- Card and advisor cooldowns stay measured in months: each turn subtracts the turn length, so a six-month cooldown is twelve turns in 1917.
- Drifting stats (bread, ruble, war weariness, land pressure) change by the turn length times their monthly rate.
- Events in 1917 can test the half-month as well as the month, so the July Days fire in the second half of July.
- The date display (`month.qdisplay.dry`) shows "Early July 1917" and "Late July 1917" until the switch, then "March 1918".
- One card per turn throughout, so 1917 gives twice the decisions per month: the most intense stretch of the game, by design.

## What carries over from the engine

The engine can be kept almost whole; the conversion is mostly new content plus three new mechanics. The original is about 14,500 lines across 173 Dendry scene files (81 events, 26 advisors, 22 government cards, 20 party cards) and about 526 game variables. It builds cleanly today with `npm install` and `npx dendrynexus make-html`.

The election code is generic: it loops over two lists, `Q.classes` (6 voter groups) and `Q.parties` (8 parties), defined in `source/scenes/root.scene.dry` and repeated in `out/html/game.js` for the election simulator. Changing those two lists changes the electorate.

## Stats and systems

Twelve tracked stats replace the original's unemployment, inflation and republic support; most start from documented 1917 conditions. Values are on a 0–10 scale unless noted. Starting values are design estimates anchored to the evidence in the last column.

| Stat | What it measures | Start (Mar 1917) | Evidence for the start and its drift |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bread | Food reaching the cities | 3 | State grain monopoly from 25 Mar 1917 (Old Style); Petrograd had 7–8 days of grain in late October and cut the ration to 3/8 lb on 7 Nov (OS) |
| Ruble | Money and prices | 6, falling | Money supply grew from 9 billion rubles in February to about 2 billion a month by autumn; the ruble lost over three-quarters of its value by October |
| War weariness | Pressure to end the war | 7 | Rises with each failed offensive; June offensive lost up to about 120 km |
| Army discipline | Will the army obey | 5 | Order No. 1 (14 Mar NS); 100,000–150,000 desertions in the first weeks |
| Land pressure | Peasant patience on land | 4 | Recorded peasant actions: 259 in May, 1,122 in July 1917 (Soviet-era data) |
| Bolshevik strength | Bolshevik organisation and appeal | 2 | About 24,000 members in February, about 240,000 by summer |
| Right threat | Officer and Kadet counter-revolution, later the Whites | 3 | Peaks at Kornilov (Sept 1917), Denikin at Orel (Oct 1919) |
| Soviet democracy | Free, multiparty soviet elections | 8 | The key stat after October; drives the best endings |
| Repression | Cheka power | 0 | Cheka created 20 Dec 1917; Red Terror from Sept 1918 |
| Legality | Party status | Legal | Expelled from the Soviet executive 14 Jun 1918, re-legalised 30 Nov 1918, mass arrests 1921 |
| Party resources | Dues and press | as original | Menshevik membership about 193,000 in Aug 1917 |
| Party unity | Overall dissent | as original | Central Committee split 12 to 11 on the Vikzhel talks |

### Voter groups (six, replacing Q.classes)

- **Industrial workers.** The Menshevik base in spring 1917; lost to the Bolsheviks in the capitals by autumn (Mensheviks 4.1% in the Moscow district elections of Sept 1917), but won back in provincial towns in spring 1918.
- **Soldiers and sailors.** About 15 million mobilised. SR and Menshevik in spring 1917, Bolshevik by the autumn (Bolsheviks 67% on the Western Front in November).
- **Peasants.** About 80% of the population and overwhelmingly SR. The Mensheviks start near zero here except in Georgia.
- **Middle strata and intelligentsia.** Clerks, teachers, zemstvo staff, cooperators. Kadet in the capitals (about 26%), moderate socialist elsewhere.
- **Railway and postal workers.** Small but decisive: the railway union Vikzhel could stop troop trains and forced the November 1917 coalition talks.
- **Minority nationalities.** Ukrainians, Georgians, Jews, Muslims and others. Menshevik strength is concentrated in Transcaucasia (27% there in November 1917).

### Parties (eight, replacing Q.parties)

Bolsheviks, Left SRs, SRs, Mensheviks, Kadets, Popular Socialists and Trudoviks, national parties (Ukrainian SRs, Dashnaks, Musavat, Alash Orda and others), and anarchists and other. The Left SRs ran on joint SR lists in November 1917, so the game splits them off in the November 1917 turn.

### Arenas

Elections happen in four places, each with its own chart, instead of one Reichstag:

1. **Congress of Soviets (1917).** Starting seats from the June congress: SR 285, Menshevik 248, Bolshevik 105, Menshevik-Internationalist 32, others about 100.
2. **City dumas (1917).** Moscow district elections in September: Bolsheviks 51.5%, Kadets 26.3%, SRs 14.1%, Mensheviks 4.1%.
3. **Constituent Assembly (November 1917).** SRs 37.6%, Bolsheviks 23.3%, Ukrainian SRs and allies 12.7%, Kadets 4.6%, Mensheviks 3.0% (Radkey's figures; other tallies differ).
4. **Provincial city soviets (spring 1918).** Menshevik–SR majorities in 19 of 30 provincial capitals with Soviet power, then annulled (Brovkin's study, which not all historians accept).

Five party currents replace the SPD's five factions, and 16 real Mensheviks become advisors. Only the Internationalists' share is sourced (about a third of delegates at the August 1917 unity congress); the other strengths are design estimates.

## Endings and achievements

Seven endings, three of them wins; the historical ending is the default if nothing changes. Each win rests on a counterfactual that historians have actually argued.

| Ending | Trigger | Result | Grounding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Kornilov's Russia | Right threat wins in September 1917 | Loss: military dictatorship | The Kornilov affair |
| White Russia | White threat reaches Moscow (1919) | Loss: White dictatorship, socialists jailed | Kolchak dispersed the SR Directory in November 1918 |
| Exile | Legality reaches "banned" and party resources run out | Historical loss: the *Socialist Courier* in Berlin | Leaders expelled January 1922 |
| One-party state | Game ends with soviet democracy low | Historical ending | NEP without political freedom |
| All-socialist government | A Vikzhel or Democratic Conference deal holds through 1918 | Win | The November 1917 talks nearly produced a Chernov government |
| Constituent Republic | The Assembly survives January 1918 and governs | Win | The Assembly met once, for about 13 hours |
| Soviet democracy | Multiparty soviets survive to 1921, with the 1919 economic program adopted early | Best win | Brovkin and Getzler on viable soviet democracy; the Menshevik program anticipated NEP |

Georgia adds a side outcome: if it survives the February 1921 invasion, any ending gains a free Georgia epilogue.

**By party.** The table above is the Menshevik set; the loss endings are shared, and each party has its own best win and historical ending.

| Party | Best win | Historical ending |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mensheviks | Soviet democracy | Exile: the *Socialist Courier* in Berlin |
| SRs | Constituent Republic: the Assembly governs and socialises the land | Prisoners of the Right: Komuch, Kolchak's coup, then the 1922 trial as an epilogue |
| Left SRs | Soviet Coalition: a multiparty Sovnarkom survives to 1921 | July 1918: the failed rising and the end of the party |

Alliance wins count for both partners: an all-socialist government is a win for every party in it.

**Achievements** (drafts, in the original's style): *There Is Such a Party* (take power in July 1917), *Vikzhel* (form the all-socialist government), *All Power to the Assembly* (the Assembly survives), *Nineteen of Thirty* (win the spring 1918 soviets and keep them), *Chto Delat'* (adopt the 1919 program before 1920), *Free Georgia*, *Dustbin of History* (the historical ending), *Komuch Without Kolchak* (an SR government in the Volga that the Whites never capture), *Seven Commissars* (the Left SR coalition outlives Brest-Litovsk) and *One Party* (the SRs never split).

## Build roadmap

Build the 1917 phase as a playable slice first, then add one phase at a time; each step ends with a game that builds and runs.

1. **Fork and rename.** Fork `aucchen/social_democracy_alternate_history` on GitHub; change `info.dry` (title, author, new IFID), `package.json` and the README; keep `LICENSE` and add a credit line for the original.
2. **Turn system.** Add the turn-length variable, the half-month marker, cooldowns measured in months, and the New Calendar event that switches to monthly turns (see Turns and time).
3. **Party select and the player slot.** Add the party-select screen, `Q.player_party`, the `you` slot in place of `spd`, and per-party `Q.factions`.
4. **Swap the electorate.** In `root.scene.dry` and `out/html/game.js`, replace `Q.classes` and `Q.parties` with the six groups and the parties above; set starting support per campaign; recolour the parliament chart.
5. **1917 slice, Menshevik campaign.** Write the 1917 events (March to the October Revolution), the Party Affairs and Coalition Affairs decks and six advisors (Tsereteli, Dan, Chkheidze, Skobelev, Martov, Liber). Delete the German events and advisors.
6. **Alliances.** Relations, the three alliance levels, joint lists and allied advisors; check the 1917 Menshevik–SR bloc plays as history.
7. **SR and Left SR in 1917.** Their starting states, advisors, party-only cards and the split.
8. **Phase switch.** The phase variable, legality and the deck swap; then November 1917 to January 1918 for all three parties (Vikzhel, the Assembly, the Left SR coalition).
9. **1918.** Brest-Litovsk, spring elections, Komuch, the expulsion, the July 1918 rising.
10. **Civil War.** The front tracker; October 1918 to December 1921; the Soviet Affairs deck.
11. **Endings and achievements** for each party.
12. **Assets.** Public-domain portraits and photographs from Wikimedia Commons, period music; rewrite `credits_images.txt` and `credits_music.txt`.
13. **Balance pass.** For each party, play the historical line and check it lands on its historical ending; then check each win is reachable but hard.
